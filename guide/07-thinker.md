# 07 — Thinker

One subject thought through with a session, round by round, until you end it.

## What it does

`/3-thinker` puts a session in the mindset to think a subject through with you. Each round ends on its answer, and waits for you. It moves only on a fact or a better argument, and names what moved it. It ends only on your word.

Use it for a subject you cannot give every instruction for. A fix you already know needs none.

## Your first try

In session 02:

```
/3-thinker which line a newcomer should type first in this folder, once he has read the README
```

Its first round came in about two minutes: a pick, then why, when it would be wrong, what follows, and the other way.

Then push back with a fact, as I did:

```
He has never seen the layer. Isn't his first line the guide's own: Read guide/00-read-me.md whole, then do what it says? Weigh the two for him.
```

It changed its answer, and named the two things that changed it.

End it in words:

```
Agreed. The thinking ends here. Nothing follows: no document, no code.
```

It wrote nothing. The three rounds took about four minutes.

## What a session reads

To answer, a session may read beyond your project. Mine read my two other accounts' config files, to see which folders they trust. Read only. Nothing in my layer limits what a session reads.

## Unproven here

- A subject one of my thinking protocols covers. None covered mine, so it reasoned from my general rules.

## Check

- Its last turn says the thinking is closed.
- `git status` shows no new file from it.
