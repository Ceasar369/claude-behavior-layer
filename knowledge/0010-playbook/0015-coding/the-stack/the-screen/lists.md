# Lists

Up: [[the-screen-MOC|The Screen]]

How a list of records reads: its rows, its columns, its views, its search and its figures.

A list holds records, one per row.

Every table sits in a box, a list included: a hairline border, rounded
corners. Its header row sits on the surface, in plain words, never capitals,
its labels dark and smaller than its cells. A hairline closes each row; the
last closes on the box.

A list reads its own width, never the window's. With room, a header row
names the columns. The total aligns right. The title takes the rest.

A title too long for its column wraps, whole. A list never cuts a title
short.

With more room, a list's date takes its own column, just before the total.
Short of it, the date sits under the total.

Narrower, no header row shows. A row stacks: title and total first; status
and its people under them.

A row leads with its status: a tag, a coloured dot and its words. The tag
wears one shape: on the surface, a hairline border.

A status too long for its column wraps inside its tag, and the tag grows with it.

The dot's colour comes from one map.

A table on a page leads with its status, as a list's row does: the tag, its
dot and its words. A status never reads as coloured words alone.

A part that holds back the reader's act wears a status badge, Blocked: the
tag's shape, solid red, its dot and its word in white.

A row that opens its record opens whole. It carries no chevron, link or
press of its own to say so.

A list splits into views, as tabs above it.

Each view shows one page, then Show more.

The tabs never show a scrollbar, at any width. Short of room, they slide
sideways.

A view names itself in words alone. It carries no count.

A list that pages carries a search. A visible label names it; it holds no
placeholder. A lens sits inside it, at its left.

It runs as the person types, within the open view.

From `mid`, it sits right of the tabs, and narrows before the tabs give up
room. Below `mid`, it sits above them, full width.

A list that exports puts Export last in its bar, at its right end.

A list's figures stand under its title's line, side by side while there is
room, with no box. Each is a muted word over its sum, bold, in display
type. One short muted line under
each sum says what it sums.

A list shows a record's files by name, one per line. A long name keeps its
first characters, three dots, its last characters, then its extension:
holiday-ph…412.pdf.
