# The Stages

Up: [[assisting-MOC|Assisting]]

How work splits into stages, and which sessions run together.

## One test

A stage holds sessions that run in parallel.

They never harm, interrupt or slow one another. That is the only test.

A session that waits on another, at any step, belongs to a later stage.

Whatever can run in parallel runs in parallel. I say when I want otherwise.

A stage holds nine sessions at most.

The nine counts the plan's missions and their helpers. Any other session I have one of them start is my own call.

[[protocol-sizing-a-stage|Sizing A Stage]] applies the test, mission by mission.

## Two kinds of plan

A plan runs in order, or it is open. I name its kind when the team starts. A plan I never named open runs in order.

In order, a stage goes out whole, once what it needs has stopped. Every mission of it is written and on its launch page first. Part of a stage never goes out. It closes once its sessions stop.

Open, a stage is a name grouping its missions. A mission goes out once I rule it, into any stage, running or earlier, as soon as a seat is free.

A mission I place nowhere joins the stage filling: the last stage, in the plan's order, holding a mission that went out. Before any went out, that is A. Once it reaches the cap, the next mission starts the stage after.

My "move on" starts the stage after at once. It is never questioned.

An open stage closes only when I close it. The plan ends only when I end it.

## A plan holds a finish list

A stage says how the work runs. The list says where it ends.

Each line is a result I can check, true once the team is done. I agree the list in the first stage.

A line starts red. It turns green on `assistant-check`'s proof, and goes out on my word alone.

A drive of the plan never ends on a red line.

Its last line is implemented and verified, unless I stop the team earlier.

A team never ends on a walk with me. A walk with me joins a plan only when I ask for one.

## Several teams

Teams may depend on each other. Any shape works: in sequence, side by side, or a trunk team whose stage ending launches branch teams.

A team launches only once what it needs exists. A started team never idles midway, waiting on another.

A team that would wait midway is cut in two teams. The second launches once its work exists.

A team done early simply closes.

A launch list holds what one waiting team needs. Each line is a result I can check. It starts red, and turns green on `assistant-check`'s proof. Every line green → that team's row goes on my launch page. In an autonomous drive, it fires with no press.

Each place two teams share has one owning team.

Only an advisor plans across teams, and only when I ask. Each advisor I ask keeps its own plan of teams. [[protocol-planning-teams|Planning Teams]] says how. A team's assistant holds its own team only.

## The plan records stages, never waits

The reasoning is the assistant's own.

The plan names each stage and its missions. Nothing links two missions.
