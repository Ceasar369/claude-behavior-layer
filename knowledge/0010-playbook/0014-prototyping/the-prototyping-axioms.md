# The Prototyping Axioms

Up: [[prototyping-MOC|Prototyping]]

What a prototype is, how it moves, where it lives, and the taste it obeys, on top of `.claude/rules/`.

## 1. A prototype tests what I am unsure of

It is a quick visual test, only for a look I have not settled. A look borrowed from another page is settled.

It is anything I can see: a screen, a component, what a backend shows.

It skips code standards, backend wiring and logistics. Its data is typed in.

## 2. An unknown is one my words name

A value my words leave out is no unknown. It takes a marked stand-in, and is built.

## 3. It shows only what my unknowns need

Of the ways to show them, the cheapest wins: the fewest minutes and tokens.

A badge's look shows the badge, in each status. Never its list.
Where a line sits in a head shows the head. Never its page.
Where a banner sits on a page shows the page. Its place is the unknown.

## 4. It moves in generations

A generation is one round of my instructions. As many rounds as I want.

## 5. Its level sets its cost

A generation agents write shows once, when every agent has reported. No turn says one has landed.

Each writer reads the real parts it reuses, whole. A digest of their signatures saves nothing. Agents read the files anyway.

## 6. Where it lives

One app holds every prototype, apart from every layer.

A variation is a route there.

It never imports another subject's files. A lock-in or removal deletes them. One broken import breaks every prototype.

It reads its frontend's look file whole, as text. It applies it only while its route is on screen, so no other subject's route wears it.

A generation is one file.
It names each variation's route, its direction, the variation it grew from, and the looks it draws: a number, a title, and for a look that competes, its group.

Band 00 serves them all, with their page. No session serves its own.

## 7. The page shows, and picks

Its page shows the prototypes, so I see them and go from one to the next.

It also picks, in two places that share one pick: under each frame, and on a page of rows. A row is one choice: the variations, or one group of competing looks, one pick each. A look with no group is shown, never picked. Only a click changes a pick or the variation in view. A feedback box takes my words.

Sending types what I picked, and whatever I wrote, into the session's own pane — the one line I would have typed there myself.

Nothing on the page decides whether that locks a variation in or opens a new generation. The session reads the line like any other, in chat.

## 8. I lock one in

Locking one in deletes every other variation and generation of that subject.

The winner stays until its real version is built.
The session that builds it takes it down, once it is live. The session that made it never does.
An abandoned winner, one no session will build, is removed only by a removal that names it a winner.

## 9. A mission uses it like any other

A later stage's mission names the winner under "Read first". The stack wins every conflict.

## 10. It enters git when I ship

The prototypes are committed like the rest. They run no lint and no typecheck.

## 11. My taste is written down

Every variation obeys its subject's own rules.

A product screen's are [[the-screen-MOC|The Screen]]: what sits inside a screen.

A page 1100 draws has its own: the page rules, atop `.claude/lib/page.py`.

My words in chat outrank them.

Each lock-in proposes new rules, from my words and the winner. Only those I agree enter the subject's own rules.

A session gives its pick only when I ask. Each For and Against names what it rests on.
