# Protocol — An Explanation Document

Up: [[clarifying-protocols-MOC|Protocols]]

When the exchange explains one subject to me, in one document.

## The parts it touches

The skill `explanation-document`, and the page that draws its file.

## The questions, in order

### 1. Who reads it?

Me, or someone I send it to.

Someone I send it to reads only names they know: no session, no page of mine, no skill. It ends with our questions to them.

### 2. What kind of subject is it?

Its kind sets the answer's picture, and how the document ends.

| Kind | Like | The answer's picture | It ends with |
|---|---|---|---|
| Our own subject | the settings page; 1100's tabs | every part, and its state | what waits on me |
| A document someone sent | a partner's deck; a co-founder's draft | its flow, laid against ours | questions for its author |
| An outside subject | SOC 2; licence checks per region | how it works, and where it meets us | what it asks of us |
| A problem | records deleted by mistake | the cause, step by step | the fix, and its proof |
| A comparison | two email vendors | the options, on the same points | what would flip the pick |

Every other ask keeps its chat shape: `clarify-summary`, `clarify-full-picture`, `clarify-explain`, `clarify-steps`, `clarify-items`, `blurb-gpt`.

### 3. What state is each part in?

Its state sets how its section shows.

| State | It means | Its section shows |
|---|---|---|
| Built | it exists in code, and runs | its mechanism, one real run walked |
| Ruled | I ruled it; nothing runs yet | the same picture; it never says it runs |
| Assumed | a session's stand-in | who assumed it, and what would prove it |
| Open | it waits on my ruling, or my hands | its question, under what waits on me |
| Outside | another party sets it | its source, and the date it was read |
| Parked | I set it aside on purpose | one line where it belongs, never a section |

A claim nothing proved is written unproven, in any state.

A chart's box wears its kind's colour, a kind of question or outcome. Each colour keeps one meaning across the document, named once in its section's legend.

## The rules every document keeps

1. The first screen holds the title, a three-sentence summary, and the top of its picture.
2. A picture leads each section, a chart or a table. Words say only what it cannot. A part with no picture is one line in its own section.
3. A fact shows once. A note never repeats the sentence above it.
4. A quote only where my exact words matter.
5. No citation inside a sentence. Sources sit at the end. A Ruled note ends with where I said it. A document built from a source file opens each section with the part it matches.
6. A section holds three sentences and two notes, at most. One short line at most sits under a picture, its title and Open lines aside.
7. Under 800 words, charts and sources aside. Five definitions at most, each a name I was never given.
8. An example names its own case: its party, and its place. A test-data name says it is test data.
9. The page numbers every section, sub-heading, chart and table. The writer types no number.
